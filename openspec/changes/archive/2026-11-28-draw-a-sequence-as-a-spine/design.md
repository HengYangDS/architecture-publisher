## Context

See [proposal.md](proposal.md). The research page `docs/research/spine.md` records the five prototype rounds, the
three committees of three and the holder's decisions, with their measures. A plate's composition is chosen in
`src/poster/compose/plates.mjs`: a view that names one is drawn so where it fits; otherwise the radial rule reads the
view, then the matrix's rule reads the tiers figure's route defects, and every other view is drawn in tiers. The
radial figure brought lines that are not horizontal or vertical and the free figure's measures (`free.mjs`).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** a view that is a pipeline is chosen for a spine once, from its model alone, and drawn so in every language
of its work where the spine can be drawn at its tiers figure's type; a plate that gives the spine up records why;
every plate the rule does not take is drawn as before.

**Non-Goals:** the matrix's choice per language, which a committee kept as it is until a language-free rule is
confirmed on views it was not chosen on; a fold whose rows all read left to right, which round 3 measured and the
round 4 committee dropped; diagonal lines in a spine.

## Decisions

1. **A pipeline by its relations, from the model alone.** Rests on: round 2's committee, which found that a test of the
   parts alone admitted fans and trees (`models`, `film`, `program`); round 3, which measured that no view has 60
   percent of its relations between neighbouring stations; round 5, which found that a bound on a station's related
   parts counted without direction cannot keep `page` and refuse `request-path`, and that a bound of three on the parts
   a station sends to does. Considered: the longest path through half the parts (round 1); a guard measured on the
   drawing, which the seats of round 4 proposed and which would choose per language. Chosen because: each clause reads
   the view, so the composition is a fact of the view; inner stations lie inside the repository because the system's
   boundary holds its own parts and no outside card, and an end of the row can stand beyond it; the bound of three was
   proposed by a seat of round 2 before it was measured; every part off the path relating to a station is the seats'
   first ask. Applies to: views that declare no tiers and name no composition, after the radial rule (a ring is the more
   special shape, and both read the view alone) and before the matrix's rule.
2. **The serpentine, its turn marked, its path heavy.** Rests on: round 4, where all three seats chose the serpentine
   for every view; round 3, where the return line crossed 12 relations between rows. Chosen because: a row read right to
   left costs a reader one turn, which an arrowhead marks; the path drawn at a hub's weight shows where it runs, and on
   a spine plate the key names that weight the main path, so a mark keeps one meaning on the plate. The path takes the
   hub line's dye, weight and arrowhead rather than the relation's own colour, as the prototype drew it, so the key's
   one sample of the main path is the line on the figure; the boundary's name is measured in its own face, not
   estimated, so labels keep clear of it in every language. Applies to: every spine; only a relation from a station to
   the next is the path, and every other line on a spine plate, a hub's included, is drawn plain.
3. **No diagonal, and the grid's margin.** Rests on: round 4, whose seats each found a diagonal on every spine and asked
   for orthogonal lines; round 5, which drew them with one bend for a branch and a Z for a relation between rows. Chosen
   because: a spine then speaks the tiers figure's line language. The margin is the grid's own row pad, so no card
   touches the frame; it costs `page` one type step, and so its plates stay in tiers. Applies to: every spine.
4. **A spine gives way as a radial figure does.** Rests on: the radial figure's fallback (its design, decision 2).
   Chosen because: one rule for both shapes; a spine the rule takes is drawn at a type step no smaller than its tiers
   figure, else the plate is drawn as the matrix's rule or tiers would draw it and records the spine refused and why; a
   spine its author names is drawn wherever one can be: a part related to no station stands above the first row or below
   the last, and the spine is refused only where its path is too short to fold (`parts`) or no step draws it. Applies
   to: the chosen and the named spine.
5. **One statement of the free figure's helpers.** Rests on: the prototype copied `borderToward`, `near` and `middleOf`
   from the radial figure. Chosen because: two figures that place cards in a free box share them from `free.mjs`.
   Applies to: `radial.mjs` and the spine's modules.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- On the six published works the rule admits one view and no plate's figure changes: the composition serves the next
  work whose view is a pipeline. The measures that chose its numbers were taken on these works alone.
- A row read right to left is a cost to a reader; the arrowhead at the turn is the only cue.
- The readers who judged the drawings are agents, not people.
