## Context

See [proposal.md](proposal.md) and the research page `docs/research/contents-and-index.md`, whose finding 8 records
what the readers of the words found. The note is one function a language in `src/edition/words.mjs`; the record
cuts its sentence about the page's name, which the class sets as one word.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- A reading note that a reader of each language reads once and understands, saying what the English says.

**Non-Goals:**

- The product's vocabulary. The Chinese word for a statement and "links to" as a verb are used across the reader
  file and the page; they are settled with a term table of the product, not in one sentence.
- The label over a work's limits and the works' own sentences, which the research page already records.

## Decisions

1. Each sentence of the note says one thing, and names what a pronoun would leave open.
   - Rests on: `docs/research/contents-and-index.md`, finding 8: three readings of the Chinese by readers other
     than the writer, of which the second and the third disagreed about one pronoun.
   - Considered: the card's file as "the file it rests on", which one reader read as the title's file; as "the
     file that component rests on", which another found to name a component before the note has spoken of one.
   - Chosen because: "the file the card rests on" repeats one word and leaves nothing open; a fourth reader
     confirmed the sentence in its paragraph.
   - Applies to: the note in Simplified and in Traditional Chinese.
2. The note names the plates as the index's own line names them.
   - Rests on: the same finding: in Spanish and in Korean "with its plates" read as plates that belong to a part.
   - Considered: leaving the shorter form.
   - Chosen because: the index's line under its title already says "the plates that draw them" in each language,
     and a note that says the same thing in the same words is read once.
   - Applies to: the note in Spanish and in Korean.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- The Spanish and the Korean were read by one reader each, and none of the four languages by a person. → The
  sentences are short and each change was proposed by its reader; the term table is where a person's reading of
  the vocabulary belongs.
