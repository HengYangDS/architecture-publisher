# Static architecture pictures: what the sources say

A research page. It records what a study of 2026-11-01 read and measured about static pictures of whole systems, what
it found and proposed, and what it left unverified. It decides nothing; how the product stands against the study is
given under "What follows".

## Question

Architecture Publisher's static picture set is one 16:9 vector picture of a whole system, made to be shown in slides
and presentations on large screens and projectors, used secondarily as a document figure, and zoomed by its readers.
What is best practice for such a picture, by the primary sources and by the owner's reference picture?

Inside it lies the question of type sizes, which the study names and answers in its section 2.

## Short answer

- One picture, one scale: three reading levels (far, middle, close), each sized for its distance; no size profiles.
- Strong pictures have one dominant structure that fits the system, bands aligned to it, and text at its subject.
- Contrast comes from lightness, never colour alone. The line hierarchy is a proposal; projector figures are modelled.
- No tool lays out such a picture: choosing and placing spine, tracks and bands stays the product's own work.
- A machine can gate size, contrast, collisions and completeness; fit to the system's organizing idea needs a person.

## What was read or measured

The study combines three independent research passes, which it describes as working from primary sources, with
measurements of the owner's reference picture. It names its sources by author, title and year, gives no address for any,
and does not say source by source how each was read. Where it does say (second-hand, blocked, paywalled, not found,
unverified, measured), the mark stands at the source below and again at the claim in the findings; this page adds no
mark of its own. Versions and dates of sources are those of the study's day.

## Findings

### Section 1: the constraints that do not change

Whatever draws it, a static picture:

| No. | A static picture                                                        | Detail                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Cannot respond                                                          | Everything it says must be visible. Its hierarchy comes from size, weight, position and contrast, never from hovering or clicking                                           |
| 2   | Is seen at sizes nobody controls                                        | A projector across a room, a laptop, a page column, a chat thumbnail, a reader's zoom. It needs one hierarchy that works at several distances at once, not several versions |
| 3   | Is spoken over                                                          | A presenter points at its parts, so the parts need names and an obvious reading order                                                                                       |
| 4   | Travels alone                                                           | In a document or a chat it must carry its title, its claim, its scope, its truth boundary and its provenance                                                                |
| 5   | Gets seconds from afar and minutes up close                             | One dominant structure carries the system's organizing idea, a few secondary structures support it, and the details stay subordinate                                        |
| 6   | Meets 16:9 screens, washed-out projectors, slide applications and print | Vector for sharpness, raster for compatibility, and contrast carried by lightness rather than hue                                                                           |

### Section 2: three reading levels in one picture

| Level  | Where                                | What reads                                                                  | Size on a 1600×900 canvas                                                                                                                    |
| ------ | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Far    | Projected, from the back of the room | The title, the dominant structure, three to five band names, the truth line | Capital height at least 0.00465 × viewing distance (NUREG-0700, 16 arcminutes): about a 24-unit font for viewers at 4 image heights, 36 at 6 |
| Middle | A screen, a page                     | Stations, tracks, band headings, relations                                  | 17 to 22.5                                                                                                                                   |
| Close  | Zoomed                               | Definitions, owners, exits, evidence                                        | 12 to 13; about a 10 px font on the 2400 px export at 100% from 60 cm (_proposal_)                                                           |

- The main story never depends on the close level.
- Each fragment keeps a station or band name nearby, so a reader zoomed into any corner knows where they are.
- Detail refines the overview and never contradicts it.
- Slide minimums give a second bound for the far level: Microsoft's 18 pt is 30 units and Kosslyn's 20 pt about 33
  when the picture fills a 960 pt slide (section 5).

**The reference, read against the levels.** By its measured sizes (under "Measurements"), the reference is made for
close reading on a laptop and for zooming. From six image heights only its title reads; its station names need
viewers within about three to four image heights.

This answers the question of type sizes without size profiles: one picture, one scale, and each level sized for the
distance it serves.

### Section 5: architecture-diagram practice and presentation

**What the diagram guides agree on.** C4's notation guide and review checklist, arc42, IBM Design Language's technical
diagrams (updated 2026-10-29) and Azure Well-Architected's design diagrams (2025-08-13). AWS and Google publish icons
and examples but no public layout guide was found. Section 14 adds that IBM's diagram web pages returned no readable
text and that the IBM points come from its architecture visualization documents of 2021 and 2022 and the IBM/ibmdiagrams
README.

- Every picture has a title that states its type and scope, and a key for every shape, colour, line and arrowhead
  that carries meaning.
- Every element is named and its type is clear; every relationship is one-way and labelled with its purpose.
- One set of conventions across all pictures; colour never the only signal; 3:1 contrast for meaning-bearing graphics.
- Several focused pictures rather than one complete one: C4's zoom levels, Azure's context, container and focused
  views, arc42's detail only where it matters.

Where they differ: C4 and arc42 prescribe no notation, IBM prescribes a full system (8 px grid, 14 px labels); the
cloud vendors centre their product icons while Azure and IBM want words beside icons; only Azure asks for a date,
author, version and change log on the picture; C4 is about static structure, arc42 gives runtime its own view.

**Projection and documents are different reading.** Talk figures are simpler, with heavier lines, larger text and
landmarks a speaker can point at; article figures can be zoomed and read slowly (Rougier et al., PLOS Comp. Biol. 2014,
rule 3; Reynolds; Duarte). Kosslyn's slide minimum of more than 20 pt, on a 960 pt slide filled by the picture, is
about 33 units on the 1600-unit canvas; Microsoft's 18 pt minimum is 30.

**Showpieces and what they share.** Minard's 1869 march with its temperature graph; _Anatomy of an AI System_ with its
21-section essay; _Calculating Empires_ (2023) as a wall, a zoomable site and an audio guide; git-flow's parallel branch
lanes (2010); the Linux kernel map's grid of 6 functional areas by 7 layers, every node a real linked identifier; AWS
reference architectures with numbered steps and a matching narrative; Microsoft's Zero Trust posters, each stamped with
the month it was updated; _The Illustrated Transformer_, opened step by step over many figures. The strong ones share:

- one dominant ordering axis (route, time, lifecycle or layer), read in seconds;
- a second dimension carried by bands or rows;
- an explicit reading order, often numbered;
- two reading distances: the shape from afar, real names close up;
- a companion text that carries the argument;
- a date.

They fail when the drawing's distortions steer decisions (drawn distance on the London Underground map influenced route
choice about twice as much as travel time, Guo 2011), when liking stands in for use (an all-curves Paris map beat the
official angular one on planning time, and ratings did not match performance, Roberts et al. 2013), when a picture
hardens into dogma (Driessen on git-flow, 2020), when only a wall can hold it, and when a catalogue of logos stands in
for how anything works (the CNCF Landscape, kept apart from its Trail Map).

| The glance test                     | One point, grasped in about three seconds (Duarte)                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Assertion headlines                 | A full-sentence claim above visual evidence beat topic-and-bullet slides on understanding and recall (Garner, Alley et al., International Journal of Engineering Education, 2013)                                                         |
| Kosslyn et al. 2012, from 140 decks | Text above 20 pt; about four units in working memory at once; direct labels rather than a key where there is room; the most eye-catching element is the most important one; every visible change reads as meaningful                      |
| Tufte                               | Slides are a low-resolution medium; walk the audience through a high-resolution handout (the wording of Tufte's principles is second-hand)                                                                                                |
| W3C                                 | Describe the visual aloud, use typefaces with even strokes that read from the back row, share the material in advance                                                                                                                     |
| Zooming without separate versions   | PowerPoint's Magnify (200 to 400% with panning) and reveal.js's zoom plugin, both needing vector or very high-resolution sources; Mayer's signalling and segmenting principles support spotlighting one region and revealing step by step |

**Document figures.** The Google developer style guide (2026-10-09): introduce each figure with a sentence; number it
"Figure N."; write the caption as a sentence; keep alt text to 155 characters or fewer and explain a dense image fully
nearby; never keep information only in the picture; prefer SVG on an opaque ground. W3C: a short alternative plus a
long description, ideally visible to everyone. FORCE11: cite the exact version, a commit when there is no release.
Lethbridge et al. (2003): high-level documentation stays useful when dated, but engineers distrust much of it, so a
revision stamp lets readers judge how far to trust a picture.

**Behaviour and structure together.** arc42's runtime view ties every scenario step to an existing building block;
Kruchten's 4+1 adds scenarios as a view that illustrates the others. The closest documented pattern to a journey drawn
across structure is the service blueprint (NN/g 2017; Bitner et al. 2007): the customer's journey along the top, lanes
for front-stage and back-stage work beneath, and named lines for the boundaries of interaction and visibility. Balosin
(InfoQ 2017) warns against mixing runtime and static elements in one channel; giving each its own channel, sequence
across and structure in bands, avoids that.

**Anti-patterns practitioners report.** Ambiguous boxes and arrows that mix programs, code and hardware (Kruchten;
Brown's "confused mess of boxes and lines"); unexplained styling and acronyms; accidental meaning in arbitrary size or
colour; nested boxes that say no more than an indented list (Hohpe; the chapters were blocked and the point is not
verified at them); icon walls; more symbol types than readers hold (Moody 2009); crossings and broken paths (Purchase
1997; Ware et al. 2002); stale pictures, which Azure says to retire and to keep under version control; decorative
layouts that suggest relationships nobody meant.

**A structure the pass recommends** (_inference_):

| Part              | What the pass recommends                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| An overview atlas | A sentence title with the system's claim and a line of scope and revision; numbered stations in at most about four groups; parallel tracks only where work runs in parallel; one meaning per dashed line; bands aligned to the stations as directly labelled definition lists; the system and truth boundaries as labelled lines; a key of at most about six entries; a footer with repository, commit or tag, date, generator version and a link to the interactive page |
| Showpieces        | One claim each, in the same conventions: context and boundary; one critical scenario as a numbered path over the blocks; layers by capabilities; the lifecycle of the core artefact; trust and evidence boundaries                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Zoom crops        | Named regions of the same picture, so presenters zoom without redrawn versions                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| In documents      | A figure number, a sentence caption with the source revision, alt text of at most 155 characters, and the interactive page as the long description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

No study was found that tests a spine-and-bands layout with software audiences. What else this pass did not verify is
listed under "What is not established".

### Section 6: typography

**Pairing.** Source Serif 4 for display, at its largest optical size and weight 600; Source Sans 3 for text; Noto Serif
CJK SC and Noto Sans CJK SC for Chinese.

- Noto Sans CJK, the design also published as Source Han Sans, takes its Latin from Source Sans, scaled about 113–115%
  (Lunde, Adobe, 2015), so mixed Chinese and English lines match.
- About 1.9 MB beyond the fonts bundled on the study's day for the two upright Latin variable fonts.
- To keep the reference's Georgia line lengths exactly: Gelasio, metric-compatible with Georgia, 169 KB.
- Closer to Avenir Next: Inter 4.1 or Nunito Sans, whose Latin does not match the Latin inside the CJK fonts.
- Weaker: EB Garamond (no optical sizes), Libre Caslon Display (one weight), IBM Plex (IBM's identity), Atkinson
  Hyperlegible Next (Latin only).

**Licences (OFL FAQ 1.1-update7, 2023).** Outlined glyphs in an SVG or PNG are artwork, outside the font licence.
Subsetting or instancing modifies the font, and a modified font that reserves its name must be renamed: "Source" is
reserved by Source Sans 3 and Adobe's Source Serif build; the Google Fonts build of Source Serif 4, Gelasio, Inter and
Noto reserve none.

**Chinese fonts.** Since the product outlines text when it builds, subsetting the bundled Noto Sans CJK SC weights only
shrinks the package; the sizes of the weights and of the two GB 2312 subsets are in the table under
"Measurements". Characters outside a subset need a fallback.

**Shaping.** opentype.js applies only basic ligatures, so it cannot pick proportional CJK punctuation (`palt`), tabular
figures (`tnum`) or regional forms (`locl`). harfbuzzjs 1.6.4 (MIT, 2026-10-22) shapes fully and returns glyph
outlines, so it could take over shaping inside the existing pipeline. Not verified: how accurately opentype.js renders
variable-font instances, and how harfbuzzjs selects features.

**Scale on the 1600×900 canvas**, steps of 4/3:

| Tier               | Size | Notes                                                  |
| ------------------ | ---- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Fine               | 13   | floor 12; 15.6 px on a 1080p screen                    |
| Label              | 17   |                                                        |
| Station or section | 30   | Microsoft's 18 pt slide minimum on a 540 pt-tall slide |
| Display            | 53   |                                                        |

Spare steps are 22.5 and 40. Printed at a 160 mm text width, the fine and label tiers come out at 3.7 and 4.8 pt,
below Nature's 5 pt minimum, so a document shows the picture on a landscape page or as a crop.

### Section 8: lines and marks

**Transit maps.** TfL's Line diagram standard (Issue 4, 2025) sizes every mark from the route line's thickness: the
route line is as thick as the station name's x-height; station ticks are 0.66 of it, square-ended; parallel lines 0.33
each, with a 0.33 gap; bends of 45° or 90°, inner radius 3×; arrowheads stand out 1.5× on each side; interchange rings
are 0.5× thick around a 2× opening.

**Technical diagrams.** IBM: a fixed set of line styles and ends; colour always with a label or line style; strong
colours for lines, pale tints only for fills; rounded corners mean logical, square mean prescribed. By section 14 the
IBM points come from IBM's architecture visualization documents of 2021 and 2022 and the IBM/ibmdiagrams README; its
diagram web pages returned no readable text.

**Proposed hierarchy** (_proposal_), with the spine width w about 4.5 units, half the x-height of a 17-unit label:

| Element                | Specification                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Main spine             | w, 45° or 90° bends, radius 3w                                      |
| Branches               | 2.25                                                                |
| Boundaries             | 1.5                                                                 |
| Leaders and band rules | 1.0–1.15, ink only                                                  |
| Evidence paths         | 1.5, dashed 4.5 on and 3 off, square ends                           |
| Station ticks          | 0.66w                                                               |
| Interchange rings      | 0.5w thick around a 2w opening                                      |
| Arrowheads             | about 4× the connector width, filled shapes rather than SVG markers |

At most four line weights, each at least 1.5× the next; one accent; everything on the grid.

### Section 11: checks, what a machine can gate and what needs a person

Readers' experiments mainly support crossings, bends, path straightness and crossing angle; standard formulas exist for
many more properties (Purchase 2002; Mooney et al., GD 2025).

**Automatable:**

| No. | Check                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | Minimum text size per level, from the farthest viewer that level serves                                                                                                                                |
| 2   | Contrast: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and meaning-bearing lines and marks, re-checked under a projector model (black raised to 1/15 of white, _proposal_) and under colour-blindness simulation |
| 3   | No distinction carried by colour alone                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 4   | No collisions: no text over text, marks or lines; no line through a mark it does not connect; clearance at least 0.25 em (_proposal_)                                                                  |
| 5   | Unambiguous labels: each label's nearest mark is its own, the next at least 1.5× farther (_proposal_)                                                                                                  |
| 6   | Crossings and bends: none on the spine; crossing angle at least 60° and at most two bends per connector (_proposal_)                                                                                   |
| 7   | Grid conformance: anchors on the declared module                                                                                                                                                       |
| 8   | One visual language: one mark per element kind, one line style per relation kind, each labelled directly or in a key of at most five entries (_proposal_)                                              |
| 9   | Completeness: a title with type and scope, the truth line, the model revision and date, every element named, every relation labelled or keyed                                                          |
| 10  | Capacity and balance: at most five top-level groups (_proposal_); sharp density jumps between neighbouring cells flagged                                                                               |
| 11  | Clutter compared with an accepted baseline (Rosenholtz et al. 2007), not a fixed cut-off                                                                                                               |
| 12  | Selection budget: elements shown against elements in the model, with every aggregate showing its count                                                                                                 |

**Needs a person:**

- Does the dominant structure fit the system's organizing idea?
- A timed task on the projected picture: can viewers name the main flow, the bands and the boundary? Tasks, not
  preference ratings, since ratings did not predict performance in metro-map studies.
- Were the right things left out or grouped, and is the truth line accurate?
- Overall coherence: parallels, alignment, the balance of type against structure.
- Does every local variation mean something?
- A look through a real projector in a lit room.

### Section 12: anti-patterns

- An inventory of boxes and arrows at equal weight.
- Pictures decoded only through a legend; colour-only categories; icon vocabularies.
- So dense that the main story needs zoom.
- Meaning that depends on hover, clicks or motion.
- Notes detached from what they explain.
- Decorative variation that readers take as meaning.
- Heavy lines on minor structure while the main flow is faint.
- Geometry that implies facts the model does not hold.
- Hairlines, pale tints and small fine serifs that vanish when projected (for muted palettes the risk is an inference).
- No scope, revision or date; inferred relations drawn as confirmed.
- One combined pass where labels push shapes around, or rigid stages with no way back.

## What follows for the product, and where it applies

The statements below were checked against the product on its `dev` branch at commit `fedb474e` on 2026-11-17.

- The study's answer on type sizes ("one picture, one scale, and each level sized for the distance it serves", no size
  profiles) is how the product works: one page of 1600 × 900 pt, no figure text under 14 px, and no profile by viewing
  condition. A derivation of sizes from viewing conditions was drafted on 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16 and withdrawn; see
  [Letter height by viewing distance](viewing-distance-and-letter-height.md).
- Section 8's hierarchy of lines was not adopted as proposed. The product has one table of line weights and patterns
  (`src/house.json`, `lines`, landed 2026-11-15): relations into a view's hub are twice as heavy, a planned part or
  relation is dashed, a retired one dotted, and the system boundary long-dashed and dotted.
- Section 7's palette was not adopted. The product has four built-in palettes (indigo, persimmon, soot, patina) and
  validates an author's own.
- Of section 11's automatable checks, the product measures balance (largest blank rectangle, blank bands, holes, centre
  of mass) and records a miss; it never draws an overlap or sets figure text under its floor, and it refuses a view
  that no layout draws and text set wider than it was measured. The others are not built.
- The "overview atlas" the study recommends in section 5 (a sentence title, numbered stations in few groups, bands
  aligned to the stations) is what the six published works' overview pages draw today, each placed by hand by its
  author in a Typst file with coordinates. The product does not generate it. See
  [the page on the figure language](figure-language.md) for what follows from that.

It applies to the print edition's figures. The interactive page is another medium with its own figures.

## What is not established

Each open point stands here once; those the study marks at a source are also marked in the table of sources.

- O'Reilly's _The C4 Model_ and Hohpe's chapters were blocked.
- AVIXA's screen-size formula is paywalled.
- Holten and van Wijk's tapered-edge result and the wording of Tufte's principles come from secondary summaries.
- Shostack's 1984 blueprint article and Alley's two-line headline limit were not verified at their sources.
- The AWS icon kit's slide rules and a current Google layout guide were not verified; no public layout guide was found.
- No museum record for the _Beethoven_ poster and no clearance values in IBM's technical diagrams were found.
- The Isomorphic Labs year and the printed size of _Anatomy of an AI System_ are unconfirmed.
- Whether Penrose 3.3.1 runs headless and repeatably is unverified.
- How Keynote and PowerPoint import markers, dashes, `<use>` and clipping; whether an SVG `<title>` is read as alt text.
- How accurately opentype.js renders variable-font instances, and how harfbuzzjs selects features.
- The projector, colour and thin-line figures come from models, not from measurements on hardware.
- Every _proposal_ threshold, set in nine places: the close level's size as about a 10 px font on the 2400 px export at
  100% from 60 cm (section 2); the hierarchy of lines (section 8); the rules for clean import into slide applications,
  which are also untested (section 9); and, in section 11, the projector model with black raised to 1/15 of white, the
  clearance of at least 0.25 em, the next mark at least 1.5× farther than a label's own, the crossing angle of at least
  60° with at most two bends per connector, the key of at most five entries and the limit of five top-level groups.
- The structure recommended in section 5 (overview atlas, showpieces, zoom crops, figures in documents) is the pass's
  inference, and so is the projector risk of muted palettes; no study was found that tests a spine-and-bands layout with
  software audiences.
- A timed task with viewers and a look through a real projector in a lit room: section 11 lists both among the checks
  that need a person, and the study reports neither as done.
- Methods the study does not give: how each source was read, beyond its marks; how the reference picture's text runs and
  sizes were read from a PNG; the tool or formula behind the contrast and OKLCH figures, and how the models of section 7
  were built; how the sizes of outlined text (section 9) and the font sizes other than the two subsets (section 6) were
  obtained; how the tools of section 10 were examined, apart from the local probe of Typst.
