# Drawing a view as a spine: five rounds and three committees

A research page. It records which views a figure drawn along one path tells the truth about, what a prototype drew
and measured in five rounds, what three committees of three seats found in its pictures, and the decision of the
owner's delegate that the shipped code measures. It decides nothing itself: the spine enters the product through the
`compositions` item's Change, whose design repeats the decision as Rests on / Considered / Chosen because / Applies to.

## Question

Some views ask for an order: a request passes a listener, a relay and an exchange in turn; a guide is built, then
written into a page. In tiers each step sits in a row of its own or beside others in one row, and the order is read
by following elbows down the figure. Can the publisher draw such a view along its path, from the model, where the
drawing tells the truth and only there, and fill its frame as it does so?

## Short answer

Yes, for a narrow shape, and on the published works for one view only, which keeps its tiers for type. A view is a
sequence when a path along its relations has at least five stations, which are at least 60% of its parts; at least half
of its relations join stations next to each other on it; no station sends relations to more than three parts; and every
other part relates to a station. Its spine is the serpentine: the path folded into two or three rows read left to right
and back, the line turning at the frame's side with an arrowhead under the turn, each relation from a station to the
next drawn as the view's heavy line, every other part beside its station, every wire across and down. On the census the
rule admits architecture-publisher `page` alone, whose spine sets type a step smaller than its tiers in both languages,
so both its plates keep tiers and record why; every other plate is as on dev.

## What was read or measured

- The census of dev: 76 plates of eleven editions of six works, 41 views, named by its manifests.
- Round 1 (2026-11-27): the 31 English views in tiers of five works, drawn as one line of stations and measured by
  the four balance gates, crossings and bends; light runs of single plates.
- Rounds 2 to 5 (2026-11-28): the line folded, its test read from the relations, its wires made orthogonal, measured
  on the plates each round's test admitted, both languages, and over the census's 76 plates.
- Three committees of three seats (an information designer, a software architect, a book and page designer), agents
  each alone, read rounds 1, 2 and 4, and the owner's delegate decided on their answers and on round 3's and round
  5's measures.
- The shipped code over the census's 76 plates, against dev's.

## Round 1: a line of stations

Spine admitted views with a directed path through at least half the parts and at least four stations, the other parts
hung as branches, and drew the path as one row across the plate, a relation between stations that are not neighbours
as an arch over or under it. Of 31 views it admitted 19 and drew 13. Against tiers on those 13: the blank gate passed
on 2 → 1, every gate on 2 → 0, crossings 3 → 17, bends 158 → 65, type larger on 3, the same on 2 and smaller on 8;
the median largest blank rose from .140 to .191.

The first committee agreed that a spine is true only for a pipeline that the view's question orders (each seat found
one: proxy `request-path`, ethos `intent`, aigw `program`); that the longest-path test admits views that are not
sequences (aigw `authority` and `readiness`, rejected by all three); and that one line across the figure leaves its
corners blank by construction (10 of 13 miss the blank band) and often shrinks the type (8 of 13). The decision took
the radial figure into a Change ([Drawing a view round its centre](radial.md)) and left the spine for a second round:
a line folded so the figure fills its frame, one arch at most, type no smaller than tiers', and a test that the view
is a sequence, its other parts branches one level deep.

## Round 2: the line folded

The test asked for four stations, every part off the path related to a station, at least 70% of the parts carried by
the path and its leaves, and few relations skipping stations; it admitted 6 of the 41 views, in both languages alike.
Two folds were drawn: the serpentine, whose rows alternate direction and turn at the frame's side, and the return,
whose rows all read left to right, joined by a line back across the figure.

| Fold           | Plates drawn | Pass every gate, tiers → fold | Median largest blank, tiers → fold | Crossings | Route defects | Type larger / same / smaller |
| -------------- | ------------ | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------- | ------------- | ---------------------------- |
| The serpentine | 12           | 1 → 0                         | .138 → .180                        | 5 → 2     | 11 → 0        | 5 / 7 / 0                    |
| The return     | 8            | 0 → 2                         | .135 → .148                        | 5 → 14    | 10 → 0        | 5 / 3 / 0                    |

The second committee agreed that the test admitted views that are not sequences: architecture-publisher `models`, a
fan; filmkit `film`, parts feeding two sinks; aigw `program`, a tree. It read ethos `intent` and proxy
`request-path` as real sequences, the serpentine's blank as a defect (10 of 12 plates miss the gate of .12, in the
corners where the line turns), and its diagonals between rows as its main cost. It asked for a test stated in the
relations: five stations, the path holding most of the parts, most relations joining neighbouring stations, no part
that several stations converge on. The decision took that test at 60% of the parts and 60% of the relations, the
return fold only, the blank removed where it falls, and the reading order marked where the line turns.

## Round 3: the test read from the relations

Measured on all 41 views, the test admitted none. No path of any view had 60% of its view's relations between
neighbouring stations: the highest were `intent` and `request-path` at 6 of 11, then architecture-publisher `page`
at 4 of 8. The clause on parts that stations converge on refused `request-path`, whose admission check the listener
and the relay both consult: a check two stations call, not a sink of the flow. The threshold had been the holder's
guess from the seats' numbers, and the measure refuted it.

The decision as revised: at least five stations, the path holding at least 60% of the parts, and at least half of the
view's relations between neighbouring stations; the converge clause dropped. It admits `page`, `intent` and
`request-path` in both languages and refuses every view the seats rejected, which have four stations or fewer or a
lower share. With the blank worked on, the serpentine passed every gate on 5 of its 12 plates (tiers 1), its median
largest blank .135 (tiers .138), with 2 crossings (tiers 5), no route defect (tiers 11) and type larger on 5 and
smaller on none; the return passed every gate on 3 of 11, with 20 crossings, 12 of them relations between stations
that are not neighbours crossing its channel.

frame's side and turns down with an arrowhead just below the turn, and as the return.

The third committee agreed on the serpentine for every view: the return's line back crosses other relations and their
labels, and it cannot draw `page`. All three read `page` as a serpentine better than in tiers in both languages (one
chain from the delivery builder through the guide to the page, its branches beside their stations, the only view of the
three whose plates pass every gate), `request-path` better in tiers (its relay is a hub with five relations, which tiers
shows as a fan and the serpentine hides behind long diagonals), and every remaining diagonal as a fault. They differed
on `intent`, and all three proposed a guard that a spine pass every gate in every language; it was not taken, since a
build of one language cannot read the other's figure and the composition is a fact of the view. The decision kept the
serpentine and asked round 5 for a bound that refuses a hub on the path, orthogonal branches with one bend at most, the
path drawn at the weight the house's tokens give a view's hub lines, and no card touching the frame.

## Round 5: the bound, the wires, the heavy path and the margin

Counted without direction, no bound on the parts a station relates to keeps `page` and refuses `request-path`: both
have a station related to five parts. Counted by the parts a station sends relations to, a bound of three keeps `page`
(its busiest station sends to 3) and refuses `request-path` (the relay sends to 4) and `intent` (planning sends to 4):
a station that drives four parts or more is a fan, the reading all three seats of round 4 gave `request-path` and one
gave `intent`. A seat of round 2 had proposed the same bound before it was measured. The decision took it.

With every wire across and down, the path at the hub lines' weight with the turn's arrowhead, and the grid's row
padding (16 px, scaled with the step, 24 px at step 4) kept between every card and the frame:

| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| `page` en         | 4, .164, blank                           | 3, .114, none                                     | tiers, for type    |
| `page` zh         | 4, .164, blank                           | 3, .114, none                                     | tiers, for type    |
| `intent` en       | 1, .191, blank, holes                    | 2, .198, blank, holes                             | the serpentine     |
| `intent` zh       | 1, .191, blank, holes, centre            | 2, .196, blank, holes                             | the serpentine     |
| `request-path` en | 2, .140, blank                           | 2, .128, blank; 1 crossing                        | the serpentine     |
| `request-path` zh | 1, .101, none                            | 3, .125, blank; 1 crossing                        | the serpentine     |

The last column is round 5's prototype, which did not yet read the bound; under the bound `intent` and
`request-path` keep tiers. No figure drew a route defect or a slanting stretch. A relation between stations that are
not neighbours bent once at most drew none of the six; with a Z between the rows allowed it drew all. At step 4
`page` falls 17 to 23 px short in height for the margin; a margin of 4 px would keep it at step 4.

The decision for the Change: the rule (five stations, the path 60% of the parts, half the relations between
neighbouring stations, no station sending to more than three parts); the serpentine with its turn marked, orthogonal
wires, the path heavy, the grid's margin; a view whose author names `spine` drawn so where it can be. On the six
published works the rule admits `page` alone, and no plate's figure changes: the measured result, not a reason to bend
the rule, since a view that is a sequence gets a spine.

## The shipped code over the census

Each of the census's 76 plates was composed from its edition's model with dev's code and with the shipped code, six
plates a light run, and the digests of the whole plate records compared. 74 are byte for byte as on dev: the 59 in
tiers but two, the 11 matrices and the 6 radial figures. The rule admits one view, architecture-publisher `page`, in
both languages. Of the other 40 views, the first clause each misses is the five stations for 20, the parts' share for
5, the relations' share for 13, and the three parts sent to for 2, ethos `intent` and proxy `request-path`; none
misses the fifth clause first, and every plate's record is the same with it as without. The spine
of `page` sets type at step 3 against its tiers figure's 4 in both languages, so both its plates keep tiers, and their
records differ from dev's only by `refused: { kind: "spine", why: "type" }`.

Drawn as an author naming `spine` would have it, the spine of `page` takes step 3 in rows of three and two and passes
every gate in both languages, its key naming the main path where the plate in tiers names the lines into the hub; that
of `intent` takes step 2 against its tiers' 1 and misses the blank and holes gates, and that of `request-path` in
English step 2, as in tiers, and misses the blank gate: round 5's measures again. The 76 plates took 145 s and 163 s to
compose in two runs of the shipped code, against 145 s on dev; the host's load differed between runs.

## What follows for the product, and where it applies

The rule is stated once, in `SPINE_RULE` beside the path it reads (`src/poster/compose/spine-path.mjs`), and reads only
the view and its model's kinds: of the directed paths whose inner stations lie inside the repository, the one that
leaves no part related to no station, then meets the most of its clauses, then has the most stations and the most
relations between neighbours, decides. The walk takes the parts in the order of their ids, so that a work's languages
read one path, and reads at most 200,000 partial paths, which no view of the six works or the tests' fixtures comes near
(the most is 89); a denser view is read only so far. The path is read only for a view the rules before the spine's leave
open, or one whose author names `spine`. In `compositionOf` (`plates.mjs`) it comes after an author's name and declared
tiers and after the radial rule, since both read the view alone and a ring is the more special shape, and before the
matrix's rule, which reads the figure in tiers of one language. The figure is drawn by three modules beside
`radial.mjs`: the fold places the cards in rows and bands and says what each relation is drawn as (`spine-fold.mjs`),
the wires route every relation across and down and set the labels (`spine-wires.mjs`), and the spine weighs the ways a
view's spine can be set and draws the best through the grid's figure drawing (`spine.mjs`); what any figure off the grid
shares, its cards, its label clearance and its measures, stands in `free.mjs`.

On a spine plate the heavy line is the path: each relation from a station to the next, the arrowheads of its turns and
the wire on from a start or to an end set in a row of its own are drawn as the table of lines draws a relation into the
hub, in any edition. A reply between neighbours and a branch beside a row's end, which run where the line runs, are
drawn at the ordinary weight, as are the lines into the hub, so that the mark means one thing; the key names it the main
path. A spine the rule takes is weighed at every type step no smaller than its tiers figure's, the gates before the
step, since it is folded to fill its frame; one its author names, at every step the plate allows, since, as for a named
radial figure, the type test is the rule's alone. A view the rule takes whose spine cannot be drawn, or would set
smaller type, is drawn as the matrix rule or tiers would draw it, and its plate records why; an author's `spine` is
drawn wherever one can be, at any type step, and warns where it cannot. A part an author's spine leaves related to no
station stands above the first row of stations or below the last, or with the parts outside in a row of their own; such
a spine is not drawn only where the fold finds that part no place, and is refused as `parts` only where its path is too
short to fold into two rows of two.

Two choices are the implementing agent's (2026-11-28), not the decision's: the path takes the colour of the hub's heavy
line with its weight, where round 5's prototype kept the line's own colour, so that the key's sample is the line drawn;
and the boundary's name, which labels keep off, is measured in its face where the prototype estimated its width. No
census plate turns on them. Five more are the Change's holder's, at its review the same day, after an independent
review: a spine its author names is weighed at every step the plate allows, as the prototype weighed it, where the
implementing agent had weighed it from its tiers figure's step down; the rule takes back the first committee's branches
one level deep as its fifth clause, every part off the path related to a station, so that a view whose spine would have
no place for a part is refused by the rule, from its model, rather than admitted and refused by its plate (of the tests'
fixture models it refuses browser-use `overview`, whose path leaves one part related to no station); only the relations
that run forward from station to station are the heavy line, where a branch outside beside a row's end and a reply
between neighbours had been drawn heavy too; the path is read only where the rules reach the spine's, in the order of
the parts' ids; and a spine an author names places a part related to no station where the implementing agent had refused
it.

## What is not established

- No person read the pictures; the seats, the prototype and the reader are agents. The round-4 decision's last step was
  taken on 2026-11-28: one reader who had not seen the work read `page`'s spine, as an author naming it would have it,
  in both languages beside its figure in tiers. The reader would show the spine in a talk for the thread its heavy line
  gives, and named what it would change first: a blank at the upper left, the figure's weight low and right; the
  Repository card close to the top edge; two arrowheads close together under the explainer page; and the heavy path
  passing by the builder's own line to the page, so the page's maker looks secondary. The figure it saw had no key,
  which the edition sets beside the figure; the plate's key names the main path and the cited files. By the measures
  that spine passes every gate.
- The rounds' measures are light runs of single plates. The published editions were built with the shipped code on
  2026-11-28, eleven of twelve as on dev, where one fails too: every plate's figure and every PDF as on dev, `page`'s
  two records adding the spine's refusal.
- No published plate is drawn as a spine, so the drawing is exercised only by the tests' small models, by views named
  `spine`, and by the plates the rule refuses for type.
- In round 5 `intent`'s largest blank rose from .143 to .198; the prototype judged it likely that its candidates are
  ordered by crossings before blank, which a measure with that order changed would show.
- Whether a reader takes the key's main path for the heavy line was not tested.
- The thresholds rest on the six works' views; no other work was drawn.
