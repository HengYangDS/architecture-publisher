# The committee of 2026-11-18 on the edition's three carriers: a record

A research page. It records what a committee was asked, what its five seats answered in two rounds, where they
differed and what the owner said before and after. It decides nothing itself: each part of the design enters the
product through its queue item's Change, whose design repeats it as Rests on / Considered / Chosen because /
Applies to. Unlike the committee of 2026-11-15, this one converged, and the owner took its design the same morning.

## Question

How do the edition's three carriers, the landscape PDF, the portrait PDF and the page in a browser, divide their
work; what does each page of the landscape edition hold, the overview and a last page above all; and how do the
pages lead to one another?

## Short answer

One model gives one spine: the work's question and claim, the overview's statement, the views with their numbers
and names, one vocabulary and one pin. The landscape edition is looked at by a room while someone speaks; the
portrait edition is read by one person and carries the words the speaker would say; the page is where a reader
questions and checks. The overview stays one page, keeps all it states and becomes the edition's map, naming by
number and as a link the plate that enlarges each of its parts. The last page is an index of the parts by name.

## Findings: where the five seats agree

The seats: an editorial and information designer, a designer of talks and teaching material, a software architect
as reader, a designer of interactive documentation, and a keeper of the owner's constraints.

1. **The sequence of the landscape edition stays**: title (the question and its answer), contents, the overview, a
   plate a view, a last page.
2. **The overview is one page and the edition's map.** It keeps everything it states. Each step and block names
   the plate that enlarges it, by number and as a link; the model states, for a block, the view it speaks of, a
   reference and never a position, size or order. From it the product can report a view the overview does not
   reach and blocks that say one thing twice; the author trims, the product moves nothing off the page.
3. **Three sizes with fixed roles** let the page be entered at three distances: the claim and the main course, the
   page's one figure, for the room; step labels and block titles with their plate numbers for a reader at a screen;
   notes and reference facts for the zoom. When a work says more, only the smallest gives way. The sizes are roles
   and the outcome of fit; no point size is a rule.
4. **A plate owes the overview one line**: its header names the part of the overview it enlarges, linked back,
   derived from the same reference. One grid for all plates: number, title, question, and the answer in one place
   on every plate; a plate without its answer is a defect.
5. **The last page is an index.** One job: find any component by name and the plates that draw it. A body: every
   component by kind, a row each, a mark under each plate that draws it, every mark a link. A foot band: what the
   edition was built from and who accepted it, with the way to the other two carriers; the colophon folds into it
   and the rows of evidence leave the contents page. During questions a room returns to the overview.
6. **The portrait edition** is the same spine read alone: one page a view, the figure laid out anew for the tall
   page, under it the reading and a table with a row for each component the figure draws; then the index. It is
   never the place for what another carrier could not hold.
7. **The page in a browser** is where a reader questions and checks. It shares the spine and no figure: the work's
   question and claim, the corrected terms, the pin, plate numbers on the sections that deepen a plate; a stable
   address for each view and part; and a check in the product that these are the model's.

Measured by the holder on the twelve editions after the first round: 9 of 82 plates do not print their view's
summary (a rule of the composer leaves a reading out where setting it would cost the figure); every part of the
holder's sample last page already stood on the title and contents pages.

## Where the seats differed

- Whether the owner is asked what "the whole" means on the overview. All five keep the blocks whole as the default.
  Three hold that the committee settles it, since it removes nothing; the keeper and the editorial designer hold
  that every fact or every topic with its plate and one line is the owner's to say.
- Whether the last page is drawn before the owner confirms its job: two would draw it with its job in a caption,
  three would say the job to him first.
- Where a component's file locations stand in the portrait edition: in each view's row, or once in a catalogue at
  the back.

The owner's answer of 08:39 took the committee's defaults: the blocks whole, the last page as the index.
